# PRUEBA DE INGLÉS — 4° Medio — FORMA B
## Examen de práctica estilo Examen Libre de Validación de Estudios (Mineduc)

**N° de preguntas:** 25 · **Tiempo:** 90 minutos · **Puntaje total:** 25 puntos.
**Materiales permitidos:** diccionario impreso. Sin celular, calculadora ni apuntes.

### Instrucciones

- Lee todos los estímulos pertinentes. Son textos ficticios de práctica, no descripciones universales de culturas ni noticias reales.
- Las **25 preguntas son de alternativas**. Marca una sola letra en cada una.
- Cada ítem vale **1 punto**, sin descuento por error. En las preguntas de opinión o de escritura, elige la opción que cumple la consigna y está justificada; no se evalúa una opinión determinada.
- Dedica unos 25 minutos a OA1, 25 a OA3 y 40 a OA4 y revisión.
- Se incluye una transcripción para practicar síntesis de fuentes. Leerla no evalúa comprensión auditiva: no hay audio en este instrumento escrito.

## OA N°1 — Comprender información relevante para un propósito específico

### Text A — Review in a Canadian youth library newsletter (preguntas 1–5)

> **Borrowed Summers: a graphic novel worth discussing**
> In a future city, residents can borrow recorded memories from a public archive. A young courier borrows a memory of a garden to understand why her grandmother misses a neighbourhood demolished before she was born. The archive labels the recording “complete”, but the courier notices that the gardeners' voices are missing. She collects their accounts and asks the archive to include them. The drawings make the absent speakers visible as blank spaces between colourful plants. Although the ending resolves the dispute too quickly, the novel asks a valuable question: who gets to decide what a community remembers? Recommended for readers aged fifteen and above who enjoy stories about technology and belonging.

**1.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which statement best expresses the central conflict?

a) A courier must win a gardening competition before summer ends.
b) A grandmother refuses to let anyone visit a public archive.
c) A memory archive presents an incomplete community history as complete.
d) A city loses the ability to grow any plants at all.

**2.** *(Alternativas — propósito y audiencia)* Which option identifies the review's purpose and intended audience correctly?

a) Purpose: to evaluate and recommend the novel, with a judgement about its quality. Audience: readers aged 15 and over interested in technology and belonging
b) Purpose: to summarise the plot of the novel without giving any opinion about it. Audience: young children who are learning to read graphic novels at school
c) Purpose: to report a real event that happened in a Canadian city. Audience: city officials who decide which neighbourhoods are demolished
d) Purpose: to give instructions for borrowing recorded memories from an archive. Audience: librarians who manage the public archive

**3.** *(Alternativas — tabla)* Which option completes the table with relevant information from the review? Technology / Courier's discovery / Action she takes

a) Borrowing recorded memories / the archive is closed to young people / she stops borrowing memories and visits her grandmother instead
b) Recorded music / her grandmother's garden still exists / she collects the gardeners' songs and sells them to the archive
c) Digital maps / the garden was never demolished / she writes a review asking residents to visit it
d) Borrowing recorded memories / the gardeners' voices are missing / she collects their accounts and asks the archive to include them

**4.** *(Alternativas — análisis de género)* Which sentence is an **evaluation**, rather than plot information, that helps identify the text as a review?

a) "A young courier borrows a memory of a garden to understand her grandmother."
b) "Although the ending resolves the dispute too quickly, the novel asks a valuable question."
c) "Residents can borrow recorded memories from a public archive in a future city."
d) "She collects the gardeners' accounts and asks the archive to include them, too."

**5.** *(Alternativas — interpretación)* How do the blank spaces in the drawings connect to the missing voices?

a) They show that the artist did not have time to finish the drawings
b) They represent the colourful plants that grew in the demolished garden
c) They make visible the people who were left out of the archived story
d) They show that the gardeners never existed and were invented by the courier

### Text B — Notice from a youth centre in Wellington, New Zealand (preguntas 6–8)

> **Whose stories are on our map?**
> On 18 July, from 2 to 4 p.m., our youth centre will host a workshop about places that have changed. Bring a drawing of a public place you remember, or make one with our materials. You do not need to bring a photograph. We will ask permission before displaying anyone's story, and participants can withdraw it later. The workshop is free; book by 15 July so that we can arrange enough tables. Contact the centre if you need an accessible workstation.

**6.** *(Alternativas — localizar para actuar)* You have no photographs and need an accessible table. According to the notice, what can you do?

a) Bring a drawing or make one with the centre's materials, and contact the centre to ask for an accessible workstation
b) Bring a photograph of a public place, because it is required, and arrive early to get one of the tables
c) Stay at home and send your story by email, because the workshop is only for people who bring photographs
d) Pay a small fee to reserve an accessible table before 18 July, and bring any drawing or photograph you have

**7.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which statement accurately compares the texts' treatment of community stories?

a) The review questions whose voices are included; the notice gives contributors control over displaying their stories.
b) Both texts ask every resident to publish a private family photograph so that the community archive is complete.
c) The notice says archived memories are always complete, while the review shows that archives never include anyone.
d) The review advertises a real workshop on 18 July, while the notice tells a fictional story about a memory archive.

**8.** *(Alternativas — organizador)* Which option completes the planning table for Text B? Event date and time / Booking deadline / Participation cost / Permission rule

a) 15 July, 2–4 p.m. / by 18 July / free / stories are always displayed, and permission is not needed
b) 18 July, all day / no booking needed / a small fee / photographs are required for every story displayed
c) 18 July, 2–4 p.m. / by 15 July / free / permission is asked before displaying, but stories cannot be withdrawn
d) 18 July, 2–4 p.m. / by 15 July / free / permission is asked before displaying a story, and it can be withdrawn later

## OA N°3 — Comprender y producir textos claros con postura personal crítica

### Text C — A proposal for a student media club (preguntas 9–16)

> **Proposal: captions before special effects**
> Our media club has one editing computer and a small equipment budget. At Tuesday's meeting, editor Lee said, “I have tested the caption tool.” Lee reported that automatic captions often confused speakers' names. Designer Sam proposed buying visual effects software to attract viewers. However, two viewers wrote that they had stopped watching our interviews because there were no readable captions. We recommend spending the first part of the budget on caption editing and asking viewers to test the results. Visual effects can wait until basic access has improved. This proposal does not prove that captions will increase our audience; it argues that existing viewers should be able to follow our work.

**9.** *(Alternativas — discurso indirecto)* Lee said, "I have tested the caption tool." Which option reports the statement correctly?

a) Lee said that I have tested the caption tool.
b) Lee said that they had tested the caption tool.
c) Lee says that they test the caption tool.
d) Lee said that they will test the caption tool.

**10.** *(Selección múltiple)* The automatic captions contained a wrong name; the club has not investigated the cause. Which sentence expresses a possible explanation without presenting it as certain?

a) The tool must make mistakes tomorrow.
b) The tool might have misidentified the speaker's name.
c) The tool can't ever produce an incorrect name.
d) The tool definitely changed the name deliberately.

**11.** *(Alternativas — estructura)* Which option identifies the recommendation and a supporting reason in Text C?

a) The club should buy visual effects software first, because Sam thinks special effects will attract more viewers to the interviews
b) The club should buy a second editing computer, because Lee tested the caption tool and said that one computer is not enough
c) The club should spend first on caption editing, because two viewers stopped watching the interviews when there were no readable captions
d) The club should stop making interviews, because automatic captions confuse speakers' names

**12.** *(Alternativas — tabla)* Which option completes the table? Available editing computers / Competing purchase / Evidence from viewers / Proposed way to evaluate captions

a) One / visual effects software / two viewers stopped watching because there were no readable captions / asking viewers to test the results
b) Two / a new camera / many viewers wrote that they liked the special effects / asking Sam to choose the best software
c) One / caption software / viewers asked for more special effects in the interviews / no evaluation is proposed
d) None / visual effects software / two viewers complained about the background music / asking Lee to test the results alone

**13.** *(Alternativas — articulación)* Which sentence combines the ideas with "although", keeping the contrast and meaning? "Visual effects may attract viewers. Readable captions are the club's first priority."

a) Although readable captions are the club's first priority, visual effects are not important at all.
b) Visual effects may attract viewers although readable captions.
c) Although visual effects attract viewers, so captions are the priority.
d) Although visual effects may attract viewers, readable captions are the club's first priority.

**14.** *(Alternativas — crítica)* What does the proposal NOT establish about audience growth?

a) It does not say how many editing computers the club has or how much money is available in the budget
b) It does not prove that the audience will grow; making existing content accessible does not guarantee more viewers
c) It does not mention any viewers' opinions about the interviews or the quality of the captions
d) It does not include a recommendation about what the club should buy first with the budget

**15.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which outline matches the proposal's structure?

a) Character description → imaginary dialogue → surprise ending
b) Equipment advertisement → price list → order form
c) Unrelated facts → no recommendation → personal greeting
d) Resource problem → alternatives and evidence → justified recommendation

**16.** *(Alternativas — propuesta escrita)* Which proposal includes a recommendation, evidence from C, a limitation and a next step, and uses reported speech and a past modal of possibility correctly?

a) "We recommend buying visual effects software first. Sam said that effects attract viewers, and two viewers might have stopped watching because the interviews looked boring. However, the software is expensive, so we may need more money. Next, we will ask Sam to choose the best program."
b) "Lee says: 'I have tested the caption tool.' The tool must have changed the names on purpose, and this is proven. So we recommend editing captions. Next, we will stop using automatic captions."
c) "We recommend editing captions first. Two viewers wrote that they had stopped watching because there were no readable captions. The errors might have been caused by unclear audio, so we cannot be sure editing will solve everything. Next, we will ask viewers to test the results."
d) "Captions is good for the club. Two viewers says they stop watching. We buy effects tomorrow, and viewers was happy. Next, we test."

## OA N°4 — Producir y comprender textos para interactuar y explorar identidad

### Text D — A student exhibition introduction (preguntas 17–25)

> Our exhibition, “Routes to Belonging”, presents maps made by students who have moved between neighbourhoods. A map may include a bus stop, a sports ground or a shop where someone first felt recognised. We do not claim that moving has the same meaning for everyone. Some contributors describe freedom; others describe loss. Each map includes a short reflection explaining how a place changed its maker's understanding of home.

### Text E — Transcript of an exhibition interview

> Interviewer: Your map centres on a bus stop rather than a house. Why?
> Student: When I moved, I thought home was only the building I had left. At the bus stop I met people who remembered my name. One driver told me that she had once felt like a stranger too. Those conversations helped, although I still miss my old street. I don't think belonging means forgetting where you came from.

**17.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which sentence best represents the student's understanding of belonging?

a) Feeling welcome in a new place requires erasing all memories of previous homes.
b) Only owning a house of your own can give someone a real sense of home.
c) New relationships can create belonging without removing attachment to an earlier home.
d) Public places like bus stops can never contribute to a person's sense of identity.

**18.** *(Alternativas — léxico en contexto)* Which caption uses "sense of belonging" correctly with a concrete detail from E?

a) "At the bus stop, people who remembered her name gave the student a new sense of belonging."
b) "The sense of belonging is the name of the bus stop where the student waits every morning."
c) "She belonging sense at the bus stop, because the driver remember her name every day."
d) "A sense of belonging means forgetting your old street, as the student did after moving."

**19.** *(Alternativas — síntesis)* Which option correctly explains a shared idea in D and E and attributes each source?

a) Both texts say that moving is always a completely positive experience, and the interview shows that the student no longer misses the old street because of the new friends at the bus stop.
b) The interview says that every student must make a map of the bus stop, while the exhibition is about bus drivers who felt like strangers when they moved to a new city.
c) Text D says that a house is the only place where people feel at home, and Text E agrees, because the student's map shows the building he left.
d) The exhibition says that moving can change how people understand home, and the interview gives this a personal perspective: the student found belonging at a bus stop while still missing the old street.

**20.** *(Alternativas — comprensión y precisión)* Which caption is faithful to E?

a) "The student no longer misses the old neighbourhood because the driver replaced all earlier relationships."
b) "New conversations at the bus stop helped the student, although the student still misses the old street."
c) "The student forgot the old street completely after moving."
d) "The driver refused to talk to the student."

**21.** *(Alternativas — adaptar registro)* Which email is polite and appropriately formal for the exhibition organisers?

a) "Hey guys!! Can I send a map of the library or what? It's the place where I felt welcome when I moved. Answer fast, OK? Bye, Camila"
b) "Give me a place in the exhibition now. My map shows the library where I felt welcome after moving. My address is 123 Main Street and my phone number is 555-0198, so send me the details there."
c) "Dear organisers, I would like to ask whether I may contribute a map to your exhibition. It would show the library where I first felt welcome after moving. Kind regards, Camila"
d) "Map. Library. Thanks."

**22.** *(Alternativas — perspectiva personal)* Which answer clearly explains whether an activity, rather than a place, could provide belonging, with an example linked to D or E?

a) "Yes. Joining a football team could provide belonging, because, like the student in E who was recognised at the bus stop, players are welcomed and remembered by others."
b) "Football is a sport that many people play in my neighbourhood, and the bus stop in E is near the football field where I usually play on Saturdays with my cousins."
c) "No, because only houses can give belonging, and the student in E proves it by putting a house at the centre of the map."
d) "Activities and places are the same thing in English, so the answer depends on the exhibition."

**23.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which conclusion avoids generalising beyond the two sources?

a) Every student who moves experiences it as a completely positive event, as the interview shows.
b) These accounts suggest that belonging can involve both new connections and continuing attachments.
c) Nobody can feel at home in more than one place, so the student must choose one.
d) The interview proves that bus stops are the only welcoming public spaces in any city.

**24.** *(Alternativas — oración y perspectiva)* A contributor says, "People welcomed me at the art club." Which sentence uses the passive **and** a relative clause correctly?

a) "People welcomed me at the art club, which helped me see myself as an artist."
b) "I welcomed at an art club who helped me see myself as an artist."
c) "The art club was welcome me, that is good for my identity as an artist."
d) "I was welcomed at an art club that helped me see myself as an artist."

**25.** *(Alternativas — reflexión de cierre)* Which reflection for "Routes to Belonging" introduces a place, explains its effect on identity, connects to BOTH D and E and ends with a conclusion, using a reported statement or past modal and a passive or relative clause?

a) "The library near my new home is a quiet place with many books and comfortable chairs. I go there every afternoon after school to do my homework. The exhibition shows maps of bus stops and sports grounds. The interview is about a student who moved. I think libraries are better than bus stops because they are quieter. In conclusion, everyone should visit a library."
b) "The library that I visited after moving became my first new place. Like the maps in the exhibition, it changed my understanding of home. A librarian told me that she had also moved as a teenager, just as the driver in the interview had. I was welcomed there without forgetting my old town. In conclusion, belonging can grow from new connections and old memories together."
c) "The exhibition is about maps of places. The interview is about a bus stop. I don't know which place matters to me."
d) "Belonging means forgetting where you came from, as the interview proves, so I never think about my old town."
