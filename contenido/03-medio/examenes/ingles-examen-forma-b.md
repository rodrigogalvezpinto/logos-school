# PRUEBA DE INGLÉS — 3° Medio (Forma B)
## Examen de práctica estilo Examen Libre de Validación de Estudios (Mineduc)

**N° de preguntas:** 25 · **Tiempo:** 90 minutos · **Puntaje total:** 25 puntos.
**Materiales permitidos:** diccionario impreso. Sin celular, apuntes ni calculadora.

### Instrucciones

- Lee los estímulos antes de responder. Son situaciones ficticias creadas para practicar; no representan a todas las personas de un país.
- Las **25 preguntas son de alternativas**. Marca una sola letra en cada una.
- Cada pregunta vale **1 punto**, sin descuento por error. En las preguntas de opinión o de escritura, elige la opción que cumple la consigna; no se evalúa una opinión determinada.
- Distribuye unos 25 minutos en OA1, 25 en OA3 y 40 en OA4 y revisión.

## OA N°1 — Comprender, organizar y comparar información central sobre otras culturas

### Texts A and B — Making things last (preguntas 1–8)

**A — A youth newsletter in Bristol, England**
> Our Saturday repair table began when a student's backpack zip broke. Instead of buying a new bag, she asked a neighbour to show her how to replace it. Now volunteers bring tools to the library once a month. Visitors work beside them: nobody simply drops an object off and leaves. There is no fee, but learners are asked to explain one repair to the next visitor. The organisers say that passing on skills matters more than counting repaired objects.

**B — A school bulletin in Valdivia, Chile**
> Our class runs a clothing exchange before the rainy season. Students bring clean jackets that no longer fit and choose another jacket without paying. Nobody has to donate something to receive one. We label sizes rather than owners' names, because asking for a coat should not embarrass anyone. Unlike the Bristol table, we do not teach repairs during the event. However, both projects help people use existing things instead of immediately buying replacements.

**1.** *(Alternativas — organizador)* Which option completes the organizer correctly? Bristol / Main activity / What learners pass on — Valdivia / Main activity / Why owners' names are omitted

a) Bristol: participatory repairs / they explain one repair to the next visitor. Valdivia: jacket exchange / so asking for a coat does not embarrass anyone
b) Bristol: participatory repairs / visitors pay volunteers to fix objects quickly. Valdivia: jacket repairs / so owners can collect their own coats later
c) Bristol: jacket exchange / learners pass on the sizes they need. Valdivia: participatory repairs / so volunteers can be thanked by name in public
d) Bristol: a tool library / learners pass on borrowed tools. Valdivia: jacket exchange / so the project can publish who donated each coat

**2.** *(Alternativas — comprensión)* Why is the Bristol project more than a repair service?

a) Because it charges a fee that pays for new tools and materials
b) Because it repairs backpacks, lamps and bicycles in the same place
c) Because visitors learn and pass on skills instead of just leaving objects to be fixed
d) Because volunteers repair objects quickly while the visitors wait outside the workshop

**3.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which comparison captures the main difference?

a) Both projects require visitors to donate clothing.
b) Bristol teaches repairs, whereas Valdivia redistributes usable clothing.
c) Valdivia charges for jackets, whereas Bristol charges for tools.
d) Bristol replaces all broken objects, whereas Valdivia teaches sewing.

**4.** *(Alternativas — completar)* Reuse a three-word structure from Text A: *"Volunteers bring tools. At the repair table, visitors ___ rather than leaving their belongings there."*

a) drop objects off
b) work beside them
c) count repaired objects
d) pay a fee

**5.** *(Alternativas — resumen)* A friend needs a jacket. Which summary of the Valdivia exchange is accurate?

a) You must donate two jackets first and then pay a small fee to choose a clean one.
b) Volunteers repair your old jacket while you wait, so you don't need to choose a new one in another size.
c) You can choose a clean jacket in another size without paying, and you don't have to donate one to receive it.
d) Only students who bring a jacket to donate can take one home in a different size.

**6.** *(Alternativas — comparación)* Which pair of sentences uses *both* and *whereas* correctly with evidence from the texts?

a) Both projects help people reuse existing things. Whereas Bristol teaches repairs, Valdivia does not teach repairs during the event.
b) Both projects charge a small fee to participants. Whereas Bristol is in Chile, Valdivia is in England and focuses on bags.
c) Both projects sell new products to the community. Whereas Valdivia repairs bags, Bristol exchanges warm winter jackets.
d) Both Bristol and Valdivia whereas teach repairs. Projects help, whereas texts both reuse things during the events.

**7.** *(Alternativas — inferencia)* Why might publishing owners' names undermine the Valdivia project's purpose?

a) Because owners' names would take too much space on the labels attached to each jacket
b) Because jackets with owners' names would become more expensive to exchange at the event
c) Because owners would see their names published and want their jackets back immediately
d) Because identifying owners could embarrass people asking for clothes, which goes against dignified access

**8.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which heading best fits both texts?

a) Why every student needs new equipment every year
b) A competition to repair the most objects in one day
c) Learning to sell used clothes and bags online
d) Community projects that extend the life of useful things

## OA N°3 — Analizar textos y construir una postura personal crítica

### Texts C and D — A quieter lunch? (preguntas 9–16)

**C — Student newspaper editorial**
> The dining hall is so loud that some students eat outside even in winter. We propose a quiet lunch room twice a week. Students could choose it without giving a medical explanation. A room with fewer conversations would give people a break before afternoon lessons. We are not asking the entire school to stop talking: choice is the point.

**D — School council response**
> We support offering a calmer space. However, the only spare room is upstairs and has no lift. Opening it as the sole quiet option would exclude some students. We suggest reserving a corner of the ground-floor hall first, then asking users whether it is quiet enough. A trial could reveal problems before a permanent decision is made.

**9.** *(Alternativas — análisis)* In Text C, what does *"choice"* refer to?

a) Choosing what food to eat for lunch from a longer and healthier menu in the dining hall
b) Being able to choose a quieter room without imposing silence on the whole school or giving a medical explanation
c) Choosing which teacher to have for the afternoon lessons after the lunch break
d) Choosing to stop all conversations in the school at lunchtime, so that every room becomes completely silent

**10.** *(Alternativas — resumen guiado)* Which summary of Text C follows the model proposal / reason / what is NOT requested?

a) Students want to close the dining hall. They think lunch is too noisy and too short. They want silence in every room of the school, every day.
b) The school must build a new quiet building next year. It would be cheap and easy. Everyone in the school already agrees with this idea.
c) Students propose a quiet lunch room twice a week. It would give people a break from the noise before afternoon lessons. They are not asking the whole school to stop talking.
d) Students propose a quiet lunch room every day. They are tired of the dining hall. They are asking the whole school to stop talking at lunchtime.

**11.** *(Alternativas — comparación)* Which option states one point of agreement and one difference between C and D?

a) Both support offering a calmer space; however, D argues the upstairs room would exclude students and proposes testing a ground-floor corner first.
b) Both reject the idea of any quiet space at lunchtime; however, C wants to install a lift in the building, while D prefers to keep the dining hall as it is.
c) C and D disagree completely about everything: C wants a calmer space, while D says students do not need any break from the noise before lessons.
d) Both want the upstairs room as the only option; however, D thinks the trial should start next year, while C wants to open it immediately.

**12.** *(Varias correctas — postura crítica)* Should the school use the upstairs room or test the ground-floor corner? Which answers give a position with a reason grounded in the texts? Marca **todas** las correctas.

a) Test the ground-floor corner first, because the upstairs room has no lift and would exclude some students.
b) Use the upstairs room only if a lift is installed, because a quiet room must be accessible to everyone.
c) Use the upstairs room because I like stairs.
d) Do nothing, because the texts are about jackets.

**13.** *(Alternativas — lenguaje en contexto)* In *"A trial could reveal problems"*, what does *could* tell us about the result?

a) It expresses certainty: problems will definitely appear
b) It expresses obligation: the council must find problems during the trial
c) It refers to a past event: the trial already revealed some problems
d) It expresses possibility, not a guarantee that problems will be detected

**14.** *(Alternativas — audiencia y propósito)* Which notice for younger students explains the council's trial clearly and invites feedback?

a) The council hereby declares the provisional implementation of an acoustic pilot in designated ground-floor areas.
b) We will try a quiet corner on the ground floor at lunchtime. Tell us if it helps you rest or if it is too noisy!
c) Upstairs room, no lift, silence at all times. Students who talk will have to leave the room immediately!
d) Stop talking in the dining hall forever. From now on, lunch will be completely silent for everyone at school.

**15.** *(Alternativas — límite de la evidencia)* The council has not yet tested its idea. Which question to users would help assess the proposal?

a) "What is your favourite colour for the room?", because the answer makes the room look nicer
b) "Do you like the dining hall?", because everyone already likes it and the answer is always yes
c) "Was the ground-floor corner quiet enough for you to rest?", because the answer shows whether the trial works
d) No question is needed, because the idea is already proven and the council knows what students want

**16.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which concluding sentence fairly represents both texts?

a) A calmer lunch option is worth exploring, but its location and effectiveness need attention.
b) Both writers want to ban conversation everywhere at lunchtime, starting next week.
c) The trial has already proved that every student prefers silence during the lunch break.
d) The council rejects any calmer space for students, because the upstairs room has no lift.

## OA N°4 — Producir textos claros para interactuar y reflexionar sobre la identidad

### Model email (preguntas 17–24)

> Hi Alex,
> Our class is creating a map of places where we feel welcome. I chose the public basketball court, although I rarely play. I help record scores, and the players have taught me that being part of a group does not require having the same skills. Visitors are invited to suggest new places for the map. Could you tell us about a place that matters to you?
> Best,
> Robin

Puedes usar experiencias reales o inventadas; no incluyas datos privados ni direcciones.

**17.** *(Alternativas — planificación)* Which plan for your reply to Robin is appropriate?

a) Audience: Robin, a classmate. Purpose: reply and share an experience. Place: a community library where I volunteer.
b) Audience: the school principal. Purpose: complain about the map Robin's class made. Place: the principal's office at school.
c) Audience: Alex's teacher. Purpose: sell a basketball to the class. Place: a sports shop near the community library.
d) Audience: Robin, a classmate. Purpose: give Robin instructions to redraw the map. Place: none, because it is not necessary.

**18.** *(Alternativas — producción)* Which two connected sentences explain how a place can shape your sense of belonging?

a) The community garden is green and full of vegetables. Vegetables are healthy, and older neighbours grow tomatoes and lettuce there every year.
b) I belong to the community garden. Garden belong me because older neighbours, vegetables, since then, I feel youngest volunteer the neighbourhood.
c) My neighbourhood has a community garden, a bakery and a bus stop. The garden is open every Saturday, and the bakery sells bread early in the morning.
d) At the community garden, older neighbours taught me how to plant vegetables. Since then, I feel I belong to my neighbourhood, even though I am the youngest volunteer.

**19.** *(Alternativas — voz pasiva)* Using *"visitors / welcome / by the group / every Saturday"*, which sentence uses the present simple passive?

a) The group welcomes visitors every Saturday.
b) Visitors are welcomed by the group every Saturday.
c) Visitors were welcomed by the group last Saturday.
d) Visitors are welcoming the group every Saturday.

**20.** *(Alternativas — oración de relativo)* Combine without repeating *place*: *"This is a place. People with different skills meet here."*

a) This is a place which people with different skills meet here.
b) This is a place where people with different skills meet here.
c) This is a place where people with different skills meet.
d) This is where a place people meet.

**21.** *(Alternativas — reflexión)* Which sentence uses the frame *"If I had not ___, I would not have ___"* correctly?

a) If I had not joined the choir, I would not have discovered that I enjoy singing in public.
b) If I didn't join the choir, I wouldn't discover that I enjoy singing in public.
c) If I had not join the choir, I would not have discover that I enjoy singing in public.
d) If I would not have joined the choir, I had not discovered that I enjoy singing in public.

**22.** *(Alternativas — revisión de registro)* A reply to Robin ends *"Send me the map NOW."* Which rewrite is a polite request suited to a friendly exchange?

a) SEND THE MAP NOW!!! I need it today, don't forget it, OK???
b) Map now or else I won't answer any more of your emails, Robin.
c) You must send me the map immediately, because I need it for my class.
d) Could you send me the map, please? I'd love to see the places your class chose.

**23.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which sentence would best connect to “I used to think only strong players belonged on the court” and show a change in self-perception?

a) The basketball court in my neighbourhood opens at nine every morning.
b) Basketball requires a ball, two baskets and at least two teams to play.
c) However, keeping scores helped me recognise that my contribution also matters.
d) Therefore, I have never contributed to the group in any way, and I never will.

**24.** *(Selección múltiple)* Which closing best fulfils Robin's invitation to exchange experiences?

a) This email contains exactly six sentences, as the teacher asked us to write.
b) I refuse to mention any place, and I will not ask you anything about yours, Robin.
c) Basketball courts usually have painted lines and two baskets at opposite ends of the court.
d) The reading club helps me feel included; what makes your court welcoming to newcomers?

**25.** *(Alternativas — correo)* Which reply to Robin includes a greeting, a place, its effect on identity, a question, a relative clause and two connectors?

a) Hi Robin! The place that matters most to me is the music room at my school. I practise guitar there after school with other students. At first I was shy, and playing with others was difficult for me. I am still learning to share the songs I love. I hope your basketball team wins its next match. Bye!
b) Hi Robin! The place that matters most to me is the music room, where I practise guitar after school. At first I was shy; however, playing with others made me feel more confident. Moreover, I learned that I can share something I love. Which place did you add to the map first? Best, Alex
c) Hi Robin! I like the music room. I play guitar. I was shy. I am more confident now. Bye!
d) Dear Sir, I hereby request that you send me the map of the places your class chose. I would also like to know which place matters most to you. Regards.
